golden hour
Artist: JVKE

Key: E major, 6/8. This is the common guitar/piano reduction of the this is what ____ feels like cut; the original piano arrangement adds extra passing tones and inner voices. Musicnotes publishes E major (PV/C in 6/8 around 188 eighths; some easy piano editions mark 3/4 at the same feel). GuitarTuna’s original-key chart and Tab4U agree on `Amaj7 – G#m – F#m7 – Emaj7` for verse and `Amaj7 – AmMaj7 – Emaj7` for the chorus. Guitar usually stays in E with no capo. Easy capo-4 `C – G – Am – F` sheets are a beginner transposition, not the record’s maj7 walk. The signature chorus color is `Amaj7` sliding to `Am` / `AmMaj7` over “golden hour.”

**Intro**

`Amaj7 – G#m – F#m7 – Emaj7`

Denser charts insert `Gm7` (or `Gaug`) as a passing chord between `G#m` and `F#m7`.

**Verse**

`Amaj7 – G#m – F#m7 – Emaj7`

**Pre-Chorus**

`Amaj7 – G#m – Gm7 – F#m7 – G#7`

The “glitter for skin” lift. Easy charts skip `Gm7` and `G#7` and stay on `Amaj7 – G#m – F#m7 – Emaj7`.

**Chorus**

`Amaj7 – AmMaj7 – Emaj7 – E7/G#`
`Amaj7 – AmMaj7 – Emaj7 – E7/G#`

Beginner sheets print `Amaj7 – Am – Emaj7`. Keep `AmMaj7` (or `Am`) on the title word.

**Outro**

`Amaj7 – AmMaj7 – Emaj7 – E7/G#`

Later hits sometimes add a brief `D#m – F7` tag; treat that as extra color on the fade.

**Form**

Intro → Verse → Pre-Chorus → Chorus → Verse → Pre-Chorus → Chorus → Chorus → Outro
