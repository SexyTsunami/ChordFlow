Fly Me To The Moon
Artist: Frank Sinatra, Count Basie

Quincy Jones arrangement of the Bart Howard standard (It Might as Well Be Swing, 1964). Key: C major, 4/4 swing. This is the common jazz-lead-sheet reduction; the original arrangement adds extra passing tones and inner voices. Learn Jazz Standards and jazz-lesson writeups agree on C major for this Sinatra/Basie cut (other famous versions sit in Db or Bb). Guitar/piano stay in C with no capo. Beginner `Am – Dm – G – C` sheets drop the sevenths and the `Bm7b5 – E7` turn.

**Intro**

`Am7 – Dm7 – G7 – Cmaj7`

Short band fill of the A-section opening.

**A section**

`Am7 – Dm7 – G7 – Cmaj7`
`Fmaj7 – Bm7b5 – E7 – Am7 – A7`
`Dm7 – G7 – Cmaj7 – F7`
`Dm7 – G7 – Cmaj7 – Bm7b5 – E7`

First ending returns through `Bm7b5 – E7` to the top. Some charts replace the third-line `F7` with `Em7 – A7`.

**B section**

`Am7 – Dm7 – G7 – Cmaj7`
`Fmaj7 – Bm7b5 – E7 – Am7 – A7`
`Dm7 – G7 – Em7 – A7`
`Dm7 – G7 – C6`

Last A uses `Em7 – A7` and cadences `C6`. The final vocal tag sometimes colors `F7 – Bb7` into `Cmaj7`.

**Solo**

`Am7 – Dm7 – G7 – Cmaj7`
`Fmaj7 – Bm7b5 – E7 – Am7 – A7`
`Dm7 – G7 – Cmaj7 – F7`
`Dm7 – G7 – Cmaj7 – Bm7b5 – E7`

Same A-section changes; Basie’s band often takes a chorus here.

**Outro**

`Dm7 – G7 – C6`

**Form**

Intro → A section → A section → B section → Solo → A section → B section → Outro
