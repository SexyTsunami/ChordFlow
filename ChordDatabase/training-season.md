Training Season
Artist: Dua Lipa

Key: F minor, 4/4. This is the common guitar/piano reduction; the original arrangement adds extra passing tones and inner voices (`Fmadd9`, `Cadd11`, `Bbm9`, `Fmsus4`). Musicnotes publishes F minor; Hooktheory calls the C7 pull F harmonic minor. Chordify sometimes labels the relative C / A-flat side because `C7` is so constant. Guitar usually uses capo 1 and plays `Em – B – Am` (chorus `Bm` for concert `Cm`). Ultimate Guitar’s capo-1 chart and E-Chords agree on concert F minor; they disagree on the chorus `Cm` vs `C7`. Keep `Cm` on the “rodeo” lines and `C7` on the dominant tags.

**Intro**

`Fm – C`

Often `Fmadd9 – Cadd11` on the record.

**Verse**

`Fm – C`

**Pre-Chorus**

`Fm – C7 – Bbm – C7`

The “really want to go there” pickup. Charts spell `Bbm` as `Bbm9` for the original voicing.

**Chorus**

`Fm – Cm`

Two-bar groups on F minor, then C minor on the “rodeo” / “overload” lines. E-Chords prints `C7` here; UG comments prefer concert `Cm`.

**Post-Chorus**

`Fm – C7`
`Bbm – C7`

The “training season’s over” tag returns to the dominant `C7`, with `Bbm` as the turnaround.

**Bridge**

`Fm – C7`
`Fm – C7 – Db – C7`

Whistle/sideline figure on `Fm – C7`. The last “tell you to go” walk uses `Db` before `C7` dumps into the final chorus.

**Outro**

`Fm – C7 – Bbm – C7`
`Fm`

Same post-chorus tag, ending on F minor.

**Form**

Intro → Verse → Pre-Chorus → Chorus → Post-Chorus → Verse → Chorus → Post-Chorus → Bridge → Chorus → Outro
