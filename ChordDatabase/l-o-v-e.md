L-O-V-E
Artist: Nat King Cole

Key: G major, 4/4. This is the common piano/guitar reduction of the 1964 Capitol cut; the original arrangement adds extra passing tones and inner voices. Musicnotes and jazz lead-sheet writeups agree on G major; the record then modulates up a half step for the solo and again for the last vocal chorus. Easy C-shape reprints sit a fifth below concert. Guitar can stay in G with no capo.

**Intro**

`G – G6 – Gmaj7 – G6`

A short tonic vamp; beginner charts sit on plain `G`.

**Verse**

`Gmaj7 – Am7 – D7 – Gmaj7`
`G7 – Cmaj7 – A7 – D7`

The spelled-out first chorus. The `D7` bar before “and” is often a break (N.C.) on the record.

**Chorus**

`Gmaj7 – Am7 – D7 – Gmaj7`
`G7 – Cmaj7 – C#dim7 – G – D7 – G`

“Two in love can make it” is the only new color: `C#dim7` (some charts spell `Gdim7`) into the `G – D7 – G` tag. The last bar of this chorus is the pivot `Ab7` into the next key.

**Solo** (up a half step)

`Abmaj7 – Bbm7 – Eb7 – Abmaj7`
`Ab7 – Dbmaj7 – Ddim7 – Ab – Eb7 – Ab`

Same form as the chorus, now in Ab. Lead sheets that stay in G through the solo are transpositions.

**Final Chorus** (up another half step)

`Amaj7 – Bm7 – E7 – Amaj7`
`A7 – Dmaj7 – D#dim7 – A – E7 – A`

Last vocal chorus in A. The tag often walks `A – A/G – A/F#` before the last `E7 – A`.

**Form**

Intro → Verse → Chorus → Solo → Final Chorus
